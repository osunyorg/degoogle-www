A few weeks ago I heard about Norwegian electronic musician Kristoffer Lislegaard and his decision to fight back against Big Tech (in this case fairly specifically, Spotify) by declining to put his latest album on any streaming platforms, instead offering it as a vinyl copy and allowing digital download through platforms like Bandcamp. I was interested to speak to Kristoffer to learn more about his story because it reminded me of my own too - someone thinking about how they can somehow take a stance against the system within the context of their own career. It might feel quite impossible (and at times, a ridiculous, self-harming proposition) but also - quite a liberating one, at least at a very personal level. Interestingly this opportunity came at a time when I personally had big doubts about the way to bring the No To Big Tech project forward. Previously I had pushed that we wouldn't use any form of Big Tech when it came to our work, but I decided the time was right to go directly against this decision - and hence uploading the interview onto YouTube (owned by Google).  This will be a story for another time but ultimately, if we hope to reach a wide audience with our views, then (sadly) we need to go where the people will consume such material. And I think this is partly what Kristoffer speaks about during this interview. He understands that if he wants to have a career, he needs people to know about his music, so he has to put himself out there.  He is active on Mastodon, and he does speak up for FOSS and other aligned, more ethical tech movements as you might expect. But he admits that he still uses Instagram, and YouTube, again to help him reach his audience. We spoke about platforms like WhatsApp and how, if we were to leave these completely, we would become very isolated. Not because alternatives don't exist (clearly, they do) but because so much of the lives of the people around us are also enmeshed within the existing infrastructure. Sadly we can't force people to ditch these - it is a slow process.  Because I don't want to embed any Google/YouTube content on this website, I have instead embedded it via the more ethical option which is Tube Free (who have happily hosted all our previous video content). And I have linked to the YouTube version below, for those who might be more comfortable viewing there. This newly created YouTube channel marks a change in direction here, and I know not everyone will like this. But I stand by the decision, because again, sadly not everyone can get off these platforms. And if we want to reach as many people as we can, then this is where we have to have some kind of presence.
